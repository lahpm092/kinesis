Performance Lab · Field Study 01

Movement is
knowledge of the field.

KINESIS reads a match the way a player does — as a living field of affordances. One camera. Segmentation, skeletons, and the ecological dynamics of twenty-two athletes, computed in place.

01 · Scale

A whole half, cut to
what actually happened.

One fixed camera watched forty-five minutes of football. Our motion pipeline finds every live passage, discards the dead time, and tracks all twenty-two players through what remains — pitch-calibrated, at laptop speed. The reel below is that half, compressed and annotated.

02 · Perception

Every body on the pitch,
segmented as it moves.

SAM 3 — Meta’s promptable concept-segmentation model — runs locally over the raw footage. Asked for “person”, it returns a mask and a persistent identity for each player, every frame, no markers, no wearables.

03 · Articulation

From silhouette
to skeleton.

Inside each mask, RTMPose recovers the articulated body — 26 joints, angles resolved per frame. This is the raw material of coordination: how a knee loads, how a torso turns before the pass arrives.

04 · Kinematics

The signatures of effort.

Pitch-calibrated trajectories yield speed, acceleration and braking load for every player — the same measures elite clubs buy hardware for, read here from light alone.

05 · Ecology

No player moves alone.

Ecological dynamics treats the team as one perceiving organism. Stretch, synchrony, dominant regions, dyadic coupling — the geometry of cooperation, drawn live from the same ten seconds of film.

06 · Projection

Metrics that can
play the game forward.

The proof that a measurement matters is that it predicts. Here the measured envelopes — top speed, acceleration, work-rate, passing share — drive twenty-two agents through open play. Pass lanes light up as affordances; every ball is traced with its angle and velocity. This is selection and training intelligence you can watch.

07 · Biomechanics

The joints that
training turns.

From the same single camera, crop a player, lift the lower-body skeleton, and read hip, knee, ankle and foot angles — and how fast they change. Relative angles from vector geometry, angular speed from finite differences, distilled into trainable features and tracked, session by session, against a prescribed regimen. No wearables.

08 · Instrumentation

The camera read the game.
Now the lab reads the athlete.

Beyond the single camera sits a menu of instruments — force plates, radar, GPS, metabolic carts, and the perception–action lab that is the real differentiator. Below, that hardware is simulated for two invented players and rolled into a game-legible character sheet: eight attributes, each a composite of the devices that measure it.

09 · Prescription

The regimen
the data prescribes.

A measurement earns its keep when it changes a decision. Rivas’ profile has a clear, trainable bottleneck — the motor answer to an elite mind. The lab spends its load exactly there, and every block names the device that both prescribes and verifies it. The projected card is what those metrics buy.

10 · Projection II

What the training buys,
played twice.

One broken play, run side by side. A through-ball is slid into the channel; the covering defender shuts the shooting lane at a fixed instant. Nothing differs between the two panels but Rivas’ own envelope — today’s, and the one the regimen projects. Whether the affordance survives is decided by the physics, not the storyboard.

11 · Foundations

Measured, not imagined.

Every number in this study descends from a published line of research in ecological dynamics and sport science.
